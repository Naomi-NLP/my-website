Introduction

Hi there👋 My name is Joy Olusanya, a Linguistics graduate and NLP Researcher with 4+ years of experience across research and engineering roles in industry and academia. I build datasets, evaluation pipelines, and multilingual NLP systems with a focus on low-resource and African languages.

My work spans Automatic Speech Recognition (ASR), Text-to-Speech (TTS), and Machine Translation (MT) systems — with Yorùbá as a central language in much of my research. I am passionate about leveraging AI to preserve languages, enhance digital inclusion, and advance AI safety and governance through impactful, community-grounded research.

I am also the Founder and CEO of the Center for Low-Resource Languages and Cultures (CLRLC) — an initiative dedicated to advancing research, building resources, and fostering community around the world's underrepresented languages. CLRLC sits at the intersection of linguistics, technology, and cultural preservation, with a particular focus on African languages.

Beyond research and engineering, I am deeply committed to community and education. I served as Workshop Chair for the CLRLC-LLMs Workshop at NeurIPS 2025 and as Deep Learning / AI Co-Lead at Data Science Nigeria. I currently serve as Training Manager at Tonative Data Academy, where I design curricula and oversee tutor recruitment. I also actively teach and mentor — delivering sessions on Introduction to AI, Data Science, NLP, and Deep Learning to learners across diverse backgrounds.

Research Interests

My research sits at the intersection of linguistics, machine learning, and cultural preservation. I am particularly drawn to problems where language complexity meets data scarcity — and where the stakes for real communities are high. I believe that building equitable AI means starting from the languages and cultures that have been historically left out of the digital world.

01

Machine Translation & Multilingual NLP

I investigate how multilingual models — such as mBART50 and MarianMT — perform across typologically diverse language pairs, with a particular focus on African languages. My work examines syntactic divergence, lexical ambiguity, and the limits of transfer learning when source and target languages differ structurally.

02

Speech & Tone Processing for African Languages

Tone is meaning. In languages like Yorùbá, the same string of consonants and vowels can carry entirely different meanings depending on pitch. I study how transformer-based ASR systems handle — or fail to handle — this tonal complexity, and how we can design models that are acoustically and linguistically aware.

03

Low-resource & African Language NLP

Most of the world's languages remain invisible to modern NLP systems. I build tools, datasets, and benchmarks for languages with little to no digital data — especially African languages that are morphologically complex, dialectally diverse, or entirely absent from mainstream training corpora.

04

Cultural & Figurative Language Understanding

Proverbs, idioms, and oral literature encode centuries of knowledge in forms that are deeply non-literal. I study how language models process culturally embedded texts — where meaning is contextual, metaphorical, and often untranslatable. Yorùbá proverbs have been a central case study in this line of work.

05

AI Safety, Evaluation & Responsible AI for Africa

I am interested in how AI safety and governance frameworks can be grounded in African realities — not imported standards but systems built with the communities they affect. This includes designing culturally relevant evaluation pipelines and advocating for inclusive benchmarking practices.

06

Dataset Creation & Linguistic Annotation

Good data is the foundation of good science. I design and build high-quality, community-informed datasets — including speech corpora, parallel texts, annotated proverb collections, and dialect-specific evaluation sets — with close attention to annotation methodology and linguistic rigour.
