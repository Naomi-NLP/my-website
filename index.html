02 — Research Interests

What I Work On &
Why It Matters

My research sits at the intersection of linguistics, machine learning, and cultural preservation. I am particularly drawn to problems where language complexity meets data scarcity — and where the stakes for real communities are high. I believe that building equitable AI means starting from the languages and cultures that have been historically left out of the digital world.

01

Machine Translation & Multilingual NLP

I investigate how multilingual models — such as mBART50 and MarianMT — perform across typologically diverse language pairs, with a particular focus on African languages. My work examines syntactic divergence, lexical ambiguity, and the limits of transfer learning when source and target languages differ structurally. I am interested in both evaluation methodology and fine-tuning strategies that improve translation quality for low-resource settings.

02

Speech & Tone Processing for African Languages

Tone is meaning. In languages like Yorùbá, the same string of consonants and vowels can carry entirely different meanings depending on pitch. I study how transformer-based Automatic Speech Recognition systems handle — or fail to handle — this tonal complexity, and how we can design models that are acoustically and linguistically aware. This extends to Text-to-Speech synthesis and the challenge of naturalness in tonal language output.

03

Low-resource & African Language NLP

Most of the world's languages remain invisible to modern NLP systems. I build tools, datasets, and benchmarks for languages with little to no digital data — especially African languages that are morphologically complex, dialectally diverse, or entirely absent from mainstream training corpora. My goal is to close the representation gap without compromising on linguistic rigour or community relevance.

04

Cultural & Figurative Language Understanding

Proverbs, idioms, and oral literature encode centuries of knowledge in forms that are deeply non-literal. I study how language models process culturally embedded texts — where meaning is contextual, metaphorical, and often untranslatable — and what this reveals about the gap between statistical pattern-matching and genuine semantic understanding. Yorùbá proverbs have been a central case study in this line of work.

05

AI Safety, Evaluation & Responsible AI for Africa

I am interested in how AI safety and governance frameworks can be grounded in African realities — not applied as imported standards but built with the communities they affect. This includes designing culturally relevant evaluation pipelines, identifying failure modes specific to low-resource and multilingual settings, and advocating for inclusive benchmarking practices that reflect a broader range of languages, dialects, and lived experiences.

06

Dataset Creation & Linguistic Annotation

Good data is the foundation of good science. I design and build high-quality, community-informed datasets for NLP tasks — including speech corpora, parallel texts, annotated proverb collections, and dialect-specific evaluation sets. I pay close attention to annotation methodology, inter-annotator agreement, and the linguistic expertise required to represent tonal, morphological, and cultural features faithfully.
